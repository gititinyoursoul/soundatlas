# Minimum-Sufficient Cohort Test

## Goal

Determine the smallest defensible set of route candidates that can carry the narrative from beginning to end without unnecessary redundancy.

This stage follows targeted gap research.

Its purpose is to stop open-ended candidate discovery and decide whether the route has enough historical, geographic, and narrative coverage to move into deep research.

## Inputs

A provisional route containing:

* a working thesis
* a larger candidate pool
* evidence gathered during the light route-shape test
* additional candidates from targeted gap research
* known gaps, overlaps, and uncertainties

## Core Principle

The goal is not to select the most interesting places.

The goal is to identify:

> **The minimum set of candidates required to explain the story defensibly.**

Every retained candidate should perform a necessary narrative function.

If two candidates perform essentially the same function, prefer the stronger one unless their contrast is itself historically meaningful.

## Process

### 1. Restate the current thesis

Begin with the latest narrative hypothesis produced by earlier research.

Treat it as provisional.

The cohort test asks whether the available candidates can actually demonstrate that thesis.

### 2. Identify the strongest candidate for each necessary narrative function

Review the candidate pool in terms of roles rather than fame or historical prestige.

Possible functions include:

* emergence
* foundational practice
* community formation
* innovation
* entrepreneurship
* dancer agency
* technological development
* transmission
* infrastructure
* divergence
* commercialization
* synthesis
* culmination
* legacy

Select the strongest candidate currently available for each essential function.

### 3. Build a provisional minimum cohort

Assemble the smallest plausible route from those candidates.

A route will often contain roughly 5–8 core stops, though the correct number depends on the story.

At this stage, avoid keeping additional candidates simply because they are historically interesting.

### 4. Test each candidate for unique contribution

For every candidate, ask:

> What disappears from the story if this candidate is removed?

Possible outcomes:

**Necessary**

Removing it creates a clear historical, geographic, or narrative gap.

**Useful but redundant**

Another candidate can perform the same function.

**Contextual**

Important for explanation, but does not require its own route stop.

**Reserve**

Potentially useful if later research weakens another candidate.

**Remove**

Does not contribute enough to justify inclusion.

This removal test is central to finding the minimum cohort.

### 5. Prefer replacement over accumulation

If gap research reveals a stronger candidate, compare it directly with existing candidates.

Do not automatically keep both.

A new candidate may:

* replace a weaker stop
* absorb another stop's narrative role
* cause an earlier candidate to become context
* reveal that an entire section of the route is unnecessary

The route should generally become more precise as research progresses, not continuously larger.

### 6. Test chronology

Check whether the minimum cohort covers the historical transformation without unexplained jumps.

Ask:

* Is the beginning represented?
* Are major transitions visible?
* Is there an appropriate endpoint?
* Are there large chronological gaps?
* Is every chronological step necessary?

A candidate may survive specifically because it provides the bridge between two otherwise disconnected phases.

### 7. Test narrative coverage

Compare the retained cohort with the thesis.

Ask whether it covers all major mechanisms named in the narrative.

For example:

* communities
* technologies
* practices
* institutions
* economic structures
* cultural exchanges
* conflicts
* transitions

If the thesis names something the cohort cannot demonstrate, either:

1. add or replace a candidate, or
2. narrow the thesis.

Do not leave unsupported breadth in the thesis.

### 8. Test community representation as historical function

Representation should follow evidence and agency rather than quotas.

Ask:

* Which communities are central to the thesis?
* How does each appear historically?
* Are they represented as actors rather than simply audiences?
* Does each retained candidate reveal a distinct mechanism?

Different communities may enter the route through different forms of agency, such as:

* ownership
* organizing
* performance
* DJing
* dancing
* technological innovation
* distribution
* audience formation

Avoid forcing every community into the same type of route event.

### 9. Test the geography

Plot only the minimum cohort.

Ask whether the reduced map still contributes meaningfully to the narrative.

Look for:

* meaningful clusters
* geographic contrasts
* movement
* different neighborhood functions
* parallel developments
* convergence
* changes in geographic center
* links between peripheral and central spaces

A minimum route should retain spatial meaning even after weaker candidates are removed.

### 10. Distinguish route stops from contextual material

Some historically important candidates may still matter without becoming stops.

Move them into:

* introductory context
* an existing stop's significance
* a network description
* an epilogue
* supporting map layers
* archival side material

This allows the route to remain concise without erasing useful historical information.

### 11. Determine the route shape

With the minimum cohort visible, describe the structure that best fits the evidence.

Possible structures include:

**Linear**
A sequence of transformations.

**Branching**
Several developments emerge from a shared context.

**Parallel**
Different communities or mechanisms develop simultaneously.

**Network**
Actors, records, technologies, and places circulate between nodes.

**Convergence**
Initially separate developments eventually meet.

**Contrast**
Different responses to the same historical conditions.

The route does not need to be purely chronological.

### 12. Re-test the thesis against the reduced route

Ask:

> Can this exact set of candidates explain the thesis from beginning to end?

If not, decide whether the problem lies in:

* the cohort
* the thesis
* the scope
* or an unresolved research gap

Do not compensate with vague narrative prose.

### 13. Tighten claims

Use the cohort test to identify claims that are broader than the current evidence.

Replace absolute claims such as:

> These places invented modern club culture.

with appropriately bounded formulations when necessary:

> These developments helped establish a new model of dance culture.

The final thesis should match what the retained evidence can reasonably support.

### 14. Assign candidate statuses

At the end of the test, classify the broader candidate pool.

Useful statuses include:

* **Core**
* **Retain**
* **Context**
* **Reserve**
* **Epilogue**
* **Remove**
* **Requires further research**

This preserves rejected candidates without allowing them to clutter the active route.

## Suggested Evaluation Table

| Candidate   | Narrative function | Unique contribution        | Removal consequence              | Final status |
| ----------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Candidate A | Emergence          | Establishes new practice   | Route loses beginning            | Core         |
| Candidate B | Transition         | Connects two phases        | Creates major chronological jump | Retain       |
| Candidate C | Similar innovation | Duplicates Candidate B     | Little changes                   | Remove       |
| Candidate D | Wider context      | Useful background          | Thesis still survives            | Context      |
| Candidate E | Culmination        | Brings mechanisms together | Route lacks endpoint             | Core         |

## Coverage Check

Before locking the cohort, explicitly test:

### Chronology

Does the route cover the major historical phases?

### Narrative mechanism

Does every major process named in the thesis have evidence?

### Community agency

Are relevant communities represented through defensible historical roles?

### Geography

Does the map reveal meaningful relationships?

### Transitions

Are important changes visible rather than implied?

### Redundancy

Does every stop add something distinct?

### Scope

Can the route remain understandable without requiring excessive context?

## Output

A completed minimum-sufficient cohort test should produce:

* the thesis being tested
* the proposed minimum cohort
* narrative role for each retained candidate
* justification for why each survives
* explicit removal tests
* candidates moved to context or reserve
* route-shape description
* chronology assessment
* geographic assessment
* community/mechanism coverage assessment
* revised thesis if necessary
* remaining uncertainties
* a decision on whether the cohort can be provisionally locked

## Possible Verdicts

### Cohort passes — provisionally lock

The minimum set covers the thesis, chronology, geography, and essential historical mechanisms.

Stop general candidate discovery and move into deep research.

### Cohort passes with one targeted gap

The structure is viable, but one clearly defined question must still be resolved.

Research only that gap.

### Cohort needs restructuring

The candidates are individually strong but do not yet form a coherent route.

Return to route-shape analysis rather than broad discovery.

### Cohort fails

The concept cannot currently be demonstrated with a defensible minimum set.

Reframe or abandon the concept.

## Stop Condition

The cohort test is complete when:

1. every retained candidate performs a necessary role;
2. removing any core candidate creates an identifiable gap;
3. redundant candidates have been removed or moved to context;
4. the thesis matches the evidence available from the retained cohort;
5. geography still contributes meaningfully;
6. no known unresolved gap is likely to fundamentally restructure the route.

At that point, candidate discovery should stop.

The next stage is **deep route research**.

## Principle

Targeted gap research asks:

> **What important part of the story are we missing?**

The minimum-sufficient cohort test asks:

> **What is the smallest defensible route that still tells the whole story?**

Deep research should begin only after that minimum route is stable enough that additional research is unlikely to replace most of it.
