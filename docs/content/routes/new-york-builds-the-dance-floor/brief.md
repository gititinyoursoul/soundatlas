# Route brief: New York Builds the Dance Floor

## Route identity

- Route ID: `new-york-builds-the-dance-floor`
- Place and period: New York City, 1970–1978
- Creator: SoundAtlas
- Intended first publication: text, map pins, and timeline only

## Central question

How did different New Yorkers change the relationship between records, sound, DJs, dancers, and social space during disco's early development?

## Thesis

Disco did not begin in one nightclub or move through one simple lineage. Private-party hosts, Black promoters, Puerto Rican dancers, club DJs, record-pool organizers, engineers, and queer dance communities built different parts of a new dance-floor culture across New York.

## Reader promise

The route follows six bounded moments in which a room, an institution, a dance practice, a DJ environment, a distribution network, or a purpose-built club changed what a dance floor could do.

## Proposed event roster

1. The Loft, 1970 — a private room becomes a musical environment.
2. Leviticus, late 1974 — Black promoters build nightlife infrastructure.
3. St. Mary's Recreation Center, 1974 — dancers develop Latin Hustle in a community setting.
4. The Gallery, 1974 — the DJ shapes music, light, sound, and crowd response together.
5. New York Record Pool, 1975 — DJs and record companies establish a feedback and distribution network.
6. Paradise Garage, 1977–1978 — a club is built around sound, dancing, and community.

## Editorial boundaries

- Do not claim a single birthplace, sole inventor, or direct causal chain.
- Present the three 1974 events as parallel developments, not sequential stops.
