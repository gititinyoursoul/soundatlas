## Synthesis method and evidence boundary

This stage compares what the seven accepted Events can demonstrate together.
It does not add historical research, change Event boundaries, approve Sources,
or write reader-facing copy. Documented relationships depend on claim-specific
evidence in step 06. Interpretive-synthesis relationships compare supported
facts without claiming a direct historical link.

Event order, section grouping, map proximity, a repeated place, or a shared
actor never supplies evidence by itself. Best of Friends remains Leviticus
context. Current product coordinates are comparison points with `unknown`
historical provenance, and media availability remains separate from rights and
Human approval.

## Parallel developments and convergence points

### Parallel developments

- St. Mary's, Leviticus, and the Gallery show three forms of collective agency
  developing in overlapping 1974 time: dancer-led community practice,
  Black-owned nightlife business, and a downtown club room shaped around a
  predominantly Black gay crowd. Simultaneity corrects a single-file chronology;
  it does not connect their participants.
- The Gallery's label-supplied records, Leviticus's press parties, and the
  Pool's requested critiques reveal several music-business interfaces in
  1974–76. Only the Pool documents organized requested critiques.
- The Loft, St. Mary's, Gallery, and Garage place dancers within different
  institutional arrangements; they are not one generic floor or stages toward
  a final room.

### Convergence points

“Convergence” here is analytic, not evidence that every earlier network merged.

- The Gallery converges club institution, Black gay dance community, technical
  design, labor, and label access in one room, but no return channel to labels.
- The Garage opening converges membership, community, construction, sound,
  resident-DJ practice, and dancing without absorbing St. Mary's, Best of
  Friends, the Pool, or their authority.
- “Heartbeat” converges a named recording, a reportedly fallible floor response,
  a resident DJ, and specialist retail without proving label or market causality.

## Final working thesis

Agent synthesis for later Human review:

> New York underground Disco was built through overlapping forms of collective
> power rather than one club lineage. Hosts and dancers shaped social rooms and
> movement practices; Black entrepreneurs and working DJs built distinct
> institutions for nightlife, promotion, distribution, and critique; and
> purpose-built clubs could turn those practices into durable community
> settings where particular records were still tested, resisted, and carried
> outward. The connections matter, but so do the boundaries: promotion was not
> feedback, membership was not ownership, and chronology or proximity did not
> make separate communities one network.

The thesis preserves Arc A plus Arc C as the primary mechanism and Arc B as a
corrective lens. The seven-Event choice adds room texture and a later musical
outcome without changing that accepted direction.

## Structure comparison

### Event-centric structure

A chronological seven-chapter route would keep Event boundaries obvious, but
four Events overlap in 1974–75. Strict chronology would make St. Mary's,
Leviticus, Gallery, and the Pool look successive and could reduce the result to
a famous-club timeline with one Bronx exception and one organization.

### Argument-centric structure

An argument-centric route can group Events by their historical work: making
collective rooms and practices, controlling nightlife and record access, and
building an institution in which one record's reception becomes visible.
Transitions must name changes of mechanism, scale, borough, and evidence, and
each Event must still stand alone.

### Selected structure

Select the **argument-centric structure**. It answers the run question, keeps
the accepted arc functions inspectable, and gives both challengers distinct
jobs. Three sections meet the route-quality standard's two-to-four-phase target
without fabricating one causal chain.

## Narrative-section records

### `section-collective-rooms-and-practices`

- Title: Making collective rooms and dance practices
- Historical question: How did New Yorkers organize spaces in which dancers,
  sound, and movement practices could shape one another?
- Narrative function: establish collective agency through a domestic party, a
  Bronx community center, and a commercial downtown room.
- Ordered Event IDs: `loft-broadway-party-1970-1974`,
  `st-marys-dance-incubator-1974`, `gallery-mercer-room-1974-1977`.
- Grouping rationale: all show dancers and room practice as forces, but their
  hosts, communities, ownership, geography, and models differ. Gallery has one
  documented Loft adaptation; St. Mary's is explicitly parallel.
- Order basis and accidental-causality check: the Loft begins first; St. Mary's
  and Gallery show distinct 1974 developments. Say “in the same period,” not
  that the Loft produced Bronx practice or St. Mary's led to the Gallery.
- Transition into and out of the section: open with who shaped collective
  dancing; leave by asking how ownership and organized label/record access
  redistributed authority beyond the room.
- Presentation implication and unresolved claim gaps: separate points without
  route lines. Gallery media rights, St. Mary's end date and movement paths,
  participant representation, and coordinate provenance remain open.

### `section-owning-nightlife-and-organizing-record-access`

- Title: Owning nightlife and organizing record access
- Historical question: Who controlled institutions connecting nightlife,
  records, promotion, and critique?
- Narrative function: compare Black entrepreneurship and collective DJ
  organization while separating promotion from requested feedback.
- Ordered Event IDs: `leviticus-opening-and-label-parties-1974-1976`,
  `new-york-record-pool-launch-1975`.
- Grouping rationale: Leviticus organized a Black-owned venue and promotional
  setting; the Pool organized working DJs around distribution and critiques.
  Best of Friends supplies context without becoming an Event.
- Order basis and accidental-causality check: Leviticus operated by December
  1974 and the Pool organized in 1975, but the order is expositional. No Pool
  participation by Leviticus or Best of Friends may be implied.
- Transition into and out of the section: shift from inside-room practice to
  ownership and access; leave by asking what changed when room, membership,
  sound, and resident-DJ practice became a longer-lived institution.
- Presentation implication and unresolved claim gaps: separate Midtown and
  Prince Street points; Best of Friends stays prose context. Leviticus's exact
  day, Pool roles and original records, represented floor authority, and
  coordinate provenance remain open.

### `section-building-and-testing-a-dance-institution`

- Title: Building a dance institution and testing a record
- Historical question: What could a purpose-built community club make possible,
  and what does a later record episode reveal about DJ and floor authority?
- Narrative function: move from institution-building to the only named-record
  outcome without turning audience response into label or market causality.
- Ordered Event IDs: `paradise-garage-buildout-opening-1977-1978`,
  `heartbeat-garage-vinylmania-1981`.
- Grouping rationale: the opening establishes the setting; “Heartbeat” revisits
  it later to show a reportedly resistant floor and specialist-retail demand.
- Order basis and accidental-causality check: the institution predates the
  episode but did not cause its response or wider circulation. The second Event
  remains an attributed account only partly corroborated by trade evidence.
- Transition into and out of the section: enter through the room built around
  sustained membership and dancing; close on collective power as real but
  partial, contested, and institution-specific.
- Presentation implication and unresolved claim gaps: revisit the Garage point
  sequentially. A Vinylmania point requires 30/30A qualification and verified
  geometry. Exact night, store sequence, participant testimony, rights, and
  coordinate provenance remain open.

   broadening beyond Siano's adaptation or Prince Street context.
2. **St. Mary's boundary and voices:** the final party date, later paths, and

6. **Garage authority:** more ordinary member/worker voices and clearer design

7. **“Heartbeat” sequence:** the night, replay count, and next-day line remain

8. **Place and presentation:** coordinate provenance, Vinylmania geometry,

   `unknown` or `not-assessed`.
