## Research method and limits

This stage tested what each selected Event can legitimately demonstrate before
synthesis. It compared contemporary reporting, institutional histories,
archival finding aids, scholarship, and clearly attributed participant
accounts. A Source is mapped to the claim it supports; its presence is not
Human Source approval. Historical-evidence confidence and media readiness are
assessed separately.

The search did not use legacy, v2, or canonical Disco prose as historical
evidence. Current seed Place records were inspected only to report actual map
state. Their point coordinates have no recorded geometry provenance, so no
coordinate is treated as historically verified. Best of Friends appears only
as context for Leviticus. No new Candidate or Event is introduced.

Confidence vocabulary:

- `high`: multiple strong Sources or a contemporary/archival Source directly
  supports the bounded claim;
- `medium-high`: strong evidence with a material retrospective, date, actor, or
  boundary limitation;
- `medium`: plausible and attributed but dependent on retrospective testimony
  or an unresolved detail;
- `low`: insufficient for affirmative route copy; and
- `unknown`: not established by the searched evidence.

Media-readiness vocabulary:

- `high lead potential`: several Event-linked media types are discoverable;
- `medium lead potential`: useful Event-linked material exists but is narrow,
  remote, or incompletely attributed; and
- `low lead potential`: no adequately linked usable lead was found.

No media is `ready for publication`: rights clearance, stable delivery,
accessible description/transcript, and Human relevance approval remain
separate requirements.

### Interpretation and route function

The Event establishes the earliest bounded example of a host, dancers, sound,
record sequencing, membership, and non-commercial social intention being
organized together. It is not a sole origin for Disco or every later club. Its
route function is to establish one influential private-party model and to make
the later shift to the Prince Street Record Pool intelligible without merging
the two places or periods.

Geographically, it is an early lower-Manhattan anchor. Its proximity to the
later Gallery and Record Pool permits comparison, not an inferred exchange.

### Risky claims, non-claims, and uncertainty

- Do not call the Loft the single birthplace, inventor, or universal model of
  Disco. The institutional Source uses strong influence language, but multiple
  scenes and practices developed in parallel.
- Do not describe inclusive attendance as Black, queer, or collective
  ownership. Mancuso controlled the residence and party.
- Do not move the Broadway Event to 99 Prince Street or claim its dancers
  automatically became formal Record Pool participants.
- The precise influence of individual attendees on later venues is supported
  unevenly; only named, Source-backed links may be stated.
- Historical-evidence confidence: `high` for identity, place, and dates;
  `medium-high` for social composition and practice; `medium` for any broad
  downstream influence claim.

### Interpretation and route function

This Event anchors dancer and community agency outside Manhattan. Its role is
not merely geographic coverage: teenagers used a recreation center to develop,
test, and carry dance practices. It prevents ownership, DJs, and labels from
becoming the only historical actors in the route.

The Event can support qualified movement context toward later clubs, but not a
precise animated route until destination Events and dates are independently
bounded.

### Risky claims, non-claims, and uncertainty

- Narrowing is required: `1977` describes the institutional exhibit's wider
  dance-history period, not a verified final St. Mary's party date.
- Do not call St. Mary's the sole birthplace of Latin Hustle, Rocking, Disco,
  or hip-hop. House parties, streets, basements, clubs, and other community
  spaces also appear in the Sources.
- The claim that the parties reduced gang violence is retrospective and should
  be attributed, not presented as a measured outcome.
- Do not merge the Puerto Rican St. Mary's history with the distinct Black
  Bronx Burning history merely because the practices were contemporaneous.
- Rubberband's death is material to the boundary but its date and circumstances
  were not independently established in this search; avoid scene detail.
- Historical-evidence confidence: `medium-high` for the summer-1974 St. Mary's
  incubator and dance-practice claims; `medium` for organizer roles and later
  movement; `unknown` for the terminal date.

context. They demonstrate organizational scale but do not create additional
Event markers without venue-specific boundaries.

### Contrary evidence and power risk

The launch article warned that DJs acting collectively could bargain over what
clubs played or rejected. Later accounts also describe internal conflict over
feedback forms and governance. The Pool cannot be narrated as a neutral sensor
that simply transmitted “the dance floor.” It was an organization with
membership rules, leverage, contested roles, and incentives.

### Interpretation and route function

This is the route's clearest non-venue infrastructure Event. It documents an
organized channel through which labels supplied records and working DJs could
provide requested critiques. It connects music-business distribution to club
practice without claiming that every dancer, DJ, or venue participated.

The shared Prince Street place connects the Pool to Mancuso's later Loft, not
to the earlier Broadway Event's exact room. Later synthesis must keep those
periods distinct.

### Risky claims, non-claims, and uncertainty

- Do not claim the Pool was the uncontested first record pool without
  qualification; the 1975 New York organization is strongly documented, but
  “first” depends on definition and retrospective accounts.
- Do not assign sole-founder credit. Sources variously emphasize Mancuso,
  D'Acquisto, Casella, and other participants.
- Do not claim a specific floor reaction changed a mix, pressing, signing, or
  release without a record-level Source.
- Do not equate working DJs' written impressions with a representative sample
  of all dancers or New York communities.
- Historical-evidence confidence: `high` for launch purpose, approximate
  scale, distribution, requested critiques, and address; `medium-high` for the
  June 2 inauguration; `medium` for exact roles and feedback-sheet content.
