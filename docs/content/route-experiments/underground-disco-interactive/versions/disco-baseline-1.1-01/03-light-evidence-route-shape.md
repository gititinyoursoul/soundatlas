## Why this test uses seven Candidates

The `baseline-1.1` target of approximately 5–8 Candidates is a light-test
boundary, not a final route count. Fewer than five would let one strong example
stand in for an entire arc and would not expose geographic or representation
bias. More than eight would push this stage toward broad Candidate expansion
before the route mechanism has survived basic evidence and map tests.

Seven is sufficient here because several Candidates genuinely test more than
one arc:

- the Best of Friends and Leviticus test Black ownership, promotion, audience
  organization, and record-company interaction;
- St. Mary's tests dancer agency and movement between community and commercial
  spaces;
- the Loft, Gallery, and Paradise Garage test how dancers, DJs, community, and
  records interact inside differently organized rooms; and
- the New York Record Pool directly tests whether club response became
  organized music-business information.

The landscape is intentionally not seven famous venues. It includes a
promoter/ownership network, a community dance practice, a record-distribution
organization, and clubs with different social and industry functions.

## Arcs tested

- **Arc A — Black Disco New York:** Can exact evidence support a Black-owned or
  Black-oriented nightlife ecosystem with meaningful geography and
  infrastructure beyond participation in canonical downtown clubs?
- **Arc B — Dancers make the scene:** Can dancer agency and movement between
  homes, community spaces, and clubs support several honest, mappable Events
  rather than one symbolic Bronx stop?
- **Arc C — The dance-floor feedback loop:** Can specific evidence link dancer
  response, DJs, pools, labels, stores, radio, or record production without
  drawing a causal loop that the Sources do not establish?

## Status vocabulary

- `advance`: the Candidate has enough light evidence and route function to
  enter targeted gap research.
- `advance-with-gaps`: the Candidate is useful, but step 04 must resolve a
  named material boundary or Source gap before cohort composition.
- `hold`: the Candidate tests an important hypothesis but lacks enough Event or
  place evidence to advance yet.
- `reject`: the Candidate does not support the active arcs. No Candidate is
  rejected at this stage.

## Spatial comparison

### Method

Six Candidates have current product-supported Place records. Their coordinates
were read from `data/seed/places.json` solely as current map evidence; existing
seed prose and Event selection were not used as historical answers. A
Haversine comparison measured straight-line separation in miles. The product
records do not yet carry geometry provenance fields, so these points are
sufficient for a light shape test but require later verification before any
publication handoff.

`best-of-friends-promotion-network` has no honest single point and was excluded
from distance calculation rather than assigned an invented headquarters.

| Pair | Approximate distance | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Broadway Loft → Gallery | 0.13 mi | Extremely tight SoHo cluster; proximity supports comparison, not causality. |
| Gallery → New York Record Pool | 0.08 mi | Club and distribution infrastructure are almost adjacent geographically. Their relationship still requires documentary evidence. |
| Broadway Loft → Paradise Garage | 0.55 mi | Four lower-Manhattan Candidates fit within roughly half a mile. A map centered on them would strongly privilege downtown documentation. |
| Record Pool → Paradise Garage | 0.44 mi | A potential club/infrastructure sequence is spatially compact, but chronology and personnel—not distance—must establish relationships. |
| Gallery → Leviticus | 1.71 mi | Leviticus creates a Midtown counterpoint without making the current mapped set citywide. |
| Leviticus → St. Mary's | 5.77 mi | The Bronx node is materially separate from both Midtown and downtown clusters. |
| Paradise Garage → St. Mary's | 7.54 mi | The strongest dancer-agency Candidate would disappear visually in a downtown-only viewport. |

### Spatial findings

- Five of six mapped Candidates are in Manhattan; four are concentrated in
  lower Manhattan. This contradicts any current claim that the landscape
  already demonstrates a citywide Black nightlife network.
- St. Mary's provides meaningful Bronx separation and an actual movement
  hypothesis, not decorative borough coverage. However, one Bronx Candidate
  cannot establish Arc B as a multi-Event route or represent Puerto Rican
  dancer agency citywide.
- Leviticus changes the map from a single downtown cluster into downtown,
  Midtown, and Bronx layers. The wider Best of Friends network could add Queens
  and Brooklyn, but only after exact component venues and Events are sourced.
- Arc C currently has a strong compact lower-Manhattan infrastructure shape.
  That may be historically meaningful because clubs, DJs, and the Record Pool
  shared people and nearby spaces, but the map must not turn proximity into a
  causal arrow.
- A point-only map is inadequate for Best of Friends as an organization and for
  movement of Hustle between community and club spaces. Step 04 must either
  resolve multiple honest points or keep those relationships in prose without
  unsupported Connections.

## Arc results

### Arc A — supported, but must become more than one club plus an archive

Best of Friends and Leviticus provide concrete evidence of Black ownership,
promotion, audience organization, and label interaction. The Smithsonian
collection confirms broader archival breadth. The mapped landscape does not
yet prove the proposed citywide network, because exact Queens and Brooklyn
Events remain unresolved and five mapped Candidates are in Manhattan.

Result: **advance-with-gaps**. Arc A should remain central, with step 04 focused
on one or two exact component-network Events rather than accumulating club
names.

### Arc B — one strong mechanism, insufficient independent breadth

St. Mary's demonstrates dancer agency, community-space meaning, and movement
between neighborhood and commercial dance settings. Dancer response also
matters at the Loft, Gallery, and Paradise Garage, but accessible evidence for
those rooms is still largely narrated through hosts, DJs, owners, or
institutions.

Result: **compare-further as a mandatory lens, not yet an equal route arc**.
Step 04 should seek one additional dancer-centered New York Candidate or direct
testimony. If it cannot, Arc B should discipline A+C rather than promise a
separate route mechanism the evidence cannot sustain.

### Arc C — strongest explicit mechanism, with causal limits

The New York Record Pool supplies a concrete institutional link among DJs,
promotional records, labels, and club response. The Gallery and Leviticus show
label interaction at clubs, and Paradise Garage supplies later club/record/
retail/radio questions. The current evidence supports a network of interaction,
not one proven closed causal loop.

Result: **advance-with-gaps**. Arc C should remain central while step 04 seeks
claim-specific feedback records and prevents a diagram from overstating
causality.

## Bias and counterevidence checks

- **Documentation bias:** Strong. Loft, Gallery, and Paradise Garage have richer
  accessible histories and images than many Black-oriented archive items.
- **Geographic bias:** Strong. Five mapped Candidates are in Manhattan and four
  cluster downtown.
- **Community tokenism:** Material risk. Arc B currently depends on one
  unusually strong Puerto Rican Bronx Candidate.
- **Ownership versus attendance:** Preserved. Mixed or predominantly Black
  attendance at the Loft or Gallery is not classified as Black ownership.
- **Decorative geography:** Avoided so far. St. Mary's has a route mechanism;
  Best of Friends remains unpointed rather than receiving an invented marker.
- **Unsupported lineage:** Material risk. Personnel, chronology, and proximity
  suggest relationships among downtown Candidates, but each influence claim
  still requires Source support.
- **Famous-club bias:** Present but visible. Three famous clubs remain because
  they test multi-arc mechanisms; step 04 must not add more merely to complete a
  canon.

## Revised provisional thesis

New York underground Disco developed through interacting forms of community
and infrastructure rather than one line of clubs: Black entrepreneurs organized
nightlife networks, dancers developed and carried practices across social
spaces, and DJs built channels through which records and floor response could
move. The light evidence currently supports the Black-network and music-feedback
mechanisms more broadly than the dancer-centered mechanism, so dancer agency
must remain a required corrective test rather than an unsupported promise of
equal coverage.

This thesis is provisional. It does not claim that every community participated
in the same network, that spatial proximity proves influence, or that dance-floor
response alone caused label, radio, retail, or production decisions.

## Step-04 gap and counterevidence questions

1. Can one exact Queens or Brooklyn Best of Friends venue be bounded with
   ownership, dates, address, actors, audience, and a distinct route function?
2. Can the Leviticus opening-year conflict be resolved with contemporary city,
   newspaper, licensing, or trade evidence?
3. Is there a second dancer-centered New York Candidate supported by dancer
   testimony and an honest place boundary, or should Arc B remain a lens?
4. Which primary or contemporary records show specific dancer or DJ feedback
   moving through the New York Record Pool to a label decision?
5. Can a specific Gallery or Leviticus label interaction be distinguished from
