## Current thesis

Agent recommendation:

> New York underground Disco grew through several forms of collective power
> that sometimes intersected but did not form one seamless network. Black
> entrepreneurs built nightlife institutions; dancers created and carried
> practices through community and club spaces; and DJs organized channels
> linking records, floor response, labels, and specialist retail. The route
> should show where these systems meet, where they remain parallel, and who had
> the authority to shape each one.

This is an authoring thesis for Human decision, not approved reader-facing copy.
It does not claim that all communities participated in one system, that every
venue belonged to the New York Record Pool, or that proximity and chronology
prove influence.

## Coverage requirements

A defensible cohort must include:

1. **Black ownership and institution-building:** at least one bounded Event
   with Black ownership and audience-organization evidence, plus honest context
   for the broader network without inventing a single network marker.
2. **Dancer and community agency:** at least one Event in which dancers and
   community space drive the historical mechanism rather than appearing only
   as consumers of a DJ or label product.
3. **A documented information channel:** one non-venue Event showing how DJs
   and labels organized record distribution and requested critiques.
4. **Different room models:** enough contrast to explain how membership,
   hosting, sound, and dance-floor practice shaped institutions without turning
   the route into a list of famous clubs.
5. **A bounded musical outcome:** preferably one record-specific Event showing
   floor response and circulation without overstating label, radio, or sales
   causality.
6. **Meaningful geography:** Bronx, Midtown, and lower-Manhattan functions must
   remain visible. Queens and Brooklyn may appear only as documented network
   context until route-sized Events and verified map points exist.
7. **Inspectable causal limits:** ownership, participation, promotion,
   organized feedback, and retail circulation remain distinct.

## Classification vocabulary

- `core`: removing the Candidate materially breaks the minimum thesis,
  chronology, geography, mechanism, or community-agency coverage.
- `enrichment`: recommended addition that supplies unique value the core cannot
  provide, at an acceptable complexity cost.
- `context`: necessary framing that is not honestly bounded as a route Event.
- `reserve`: plausible challenger retained for substitution or expansion but
  not recommended in the default cohort.
- `reject`: does not warrant further route work in this version.

All classifications are Agent recommendations. They are not Human editorial
states and do not promote anything into canonical route content.

## Pass 1 — Minimum defensible core

The minimum core contains five Events:

1. `loft-broadway-house-party`
2. `st-marys-latin-hustle-incubator`
3. `leviticus-black-owned-club-opening`
4. `new-york-record-pool-launch`
5. `paradise-garage-construction-opening`

This is the smallest cohort that preserves all three selected perspectives:

- the Loft supplies the earliest bounded social/sound model and the later
  Prince Street context needed to understand why the Record Pool could form;
- St. Mary's prevents the route from reducing dance history to downtown DJs,
  owners, and labels;
- Leviticus supplies Black ownership, Midtown geography, and a label-promotion
  interface distinct from formal feedback;
- the Record Pool supplies the clearest documented two-way industry
  infrastructure; and
- the Garage opening shows a later membership institution built around Black
  gay community, dancers, sound, and a resident DJ.

The core is not a continuous lineage. Events overlap in 1974–77 and sometimes
develop in parallel. Later synthesis must not use chronological order as proof
that one Event caused the next.

## Pass 2 — Enrichment and challengers

### Recommended enrichment: `heartbeat-garage-retail-circulation`

- New value: extends the route from institutional formation in 1978 to a
  bounded 1981 record/floor/store episode with a named musical artifact.
- Why the core cannot supply it: the Garage opening cannot honestly absorb a
  later record's release, repeated play, initial dancer rejection, and reported
  Vinyl Mania demand. The Record Pool shows an organized channel but not this
  record-specific audience experience.
- Coverage gained: chronology, musical specificity, dancer response, retail
  circulation, experiential and media potential, and a useful case where the
  floor initially says “no.”
- Complexity cost: repeats the Paradise Garage as a primary place; adds a
  secondary store point whose exact address is unknown; relies on retrospective
  testimony for the floor/store sequence; requires explicit prohibitions on
  radio, national-sales, and label-causality claims.
- Recommendation: include. Its new mechanism and 1981 endpoint outweigh the
  repetition cost in a six-Event route.

### Reserve challenger: `gallery-mercer-dj-dancer-response`

- New value: adds the strongest single room-level overlap among a predominantly
  Black gay crowd, DJ attention to dancers, sound/lighting design, and free
  label records.
- Why the core cannot fully supply it: the Loft has a different private social
  model, Leviticus has Black ownership and press parties, and the Garage has a
  later purpose-built membership model. None combines the Gallery's exact
  audience, room, DJ, and promotional-access configuration.
- Coverage gained: community and room-design specificity, mid-1970s pacing,
  strong visual leads, and a direct contrast among Loft, Gallery, and Garage.
- Complexity cost: adds another famous lower-Manhattan club only 0.13 miles
  from the Broadway Loft and 0.08 miles from the later Record Pool point; adds
  no borough coverage; increases room-model redundancy; and requires repeated
  warnings that free records and dancer observation do not prove label
  feedback.
- Recommendation: retain as the first expansion challenger, but do not include
  in the default six-Event cohort. Include it only if the Human prefers a
  seven-stop route with more room-level texture and accepts the downtown-density
  cost.

### Context test: `best-of-friends-promotion-network`

- New value: makes Leviticus legible as one node in a Black-owned network with
  documented Queens, Manhattan, and Brooklyn venues.
- Why no core Event can supply it: Leviticus alone cannot establish the
  multi-borough ownership pattern.
- Complexity cost as an Event: the Candidate covers 1966–78, has multiple
  places, and lacks venue-specific dates, audiences, and mechanisms. One marker
  or stop would misrepresent the evidence.
- Recommendation: retain as contextual framing attached to Leviticus, not as a
  route Event. Deep research may later test Lucifer's or Orpheus as a separately
  bounded Candidate, but this version must not promote that possibility
  automatically.

### Challenger comparison: Gallery versus “Heartbeat”

If only one addition is allowed, prefer `heartbeat-garage-retail-circulation`:

| Criterion | Gallery | “Heartbeat” | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New chronology | Remains within 1974–77 core overlap | Extends to 1981 | “Heartbeat” gives the route a later outcome. |
| New geography | Another lower-Manhattan point | Repeats Garage plus unresolved nearby store | Neither fixes borough coverage. |
| New mechanism | Room-level audience/DJ/design/promotion interaction | Record-specific floor resistance and retail demand | “Heartbeat” adds the more distinct mechanism. |
| Community agency | Predominantly Black gay crowd, but limited direct dancer testimony | Named witness plus active, initially resistant floor | Both help; “Heartbeat” makes response narratively consequential. |
| Evidence risk | Strong room evidence; no return channel to labels | Retrospective sequence; exact night/store point unknown | Different risks, both manageable if claims stay bounded. |
| Experience value | Strong images, lighting, sound, documentary leads | One recognizable record and a dramatic play/response sequence | “Heartbeat” supplies the clearer musical endpoint. |
| Complexity | Adds a third early downtown room comparison | Repeats the Garage at a different time and function | Gallery has greater redundancy; “Heartbeat” has greater place repetition. |

### Substitution test: Gallery for the Loft

Not recommended. The substitution would strengthen the mid-1970s room-level
A+B+C overlap, but it would remove the earliest bounded party model and weaken
the historical context for the Prince Street Record Pool. The Gallery is a
valid addition, not an equivalent replacement.
