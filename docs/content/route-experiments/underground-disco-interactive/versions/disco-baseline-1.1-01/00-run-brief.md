## Fixed topic and test question

- Topic: New York underground Disco
- Place: New York City; nearby places may appear only when evidence shows a
  direct New York relationship that the route needs to explain
- Exploration period: approximately 1970–1987; step 02 may recommend a narrower
  period when an arc cannot support the full range honestly
- Research question: Which historical mechanism gives SoundAtlas the clearest,
  most place-legible account of how New Yorkers changed collective dancing,
  recorded music, nightlife, and the music business during the underground
  Disco era?
- Run test: Whether `baseline-1.1` produces a broader, more inspectable arc and
  Candidate decision than the legacy process without reproducing its route as
  an answer

## Scope

This version explores competing narrative arcs, tests the Human-selected or
authorized shortlist with one bounded Candidate landscape, researches the
material gaps exposed by that test, and recommends a defensible cohort. The
authorized terminal stage is step 05.

The route-length hypothesis is six to nine Events. This is a comparison range,
not a quota. A shorter or longer result is valid when step 05 explains the
historical, geographic, experiential, and complexity tradeoff. The intended
content depth is a medium route whose eventual major Event chapters could carry
roughly 150–220 words, but no reader-facing copy is written in this slice.

## Method hypotheses

- Four to eight consistently specified arcs will make the Human direction
  choice more explicit than an early single-thesis funnel.
- A single 5–8 Candidate light-test landscape can compare shortlisted arcs
  without multiplying research mechanically.
- Verified spatial evidence at step 03 may distinguish a genuine citywide
  mechanism from a set of famous Manhattan venues.
- Separate minimum-core and enrichment/challenger passes at step 05 may retain
  valuable community, musical, geographic, or experiential evidence that a
  compression-only pass would lose.
- Stable Candidate identities and status history should make the live run
  easier to audit and compare than the legacy checkpoint.
