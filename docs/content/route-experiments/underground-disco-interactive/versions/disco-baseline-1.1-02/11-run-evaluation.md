## Narrative and reader-first findings

Observed in `-02`:

- Step 07 made chapter structure an explicit decision before prose. Seven
  blueprints required one reader question, a supported opening, ordered beats,
  reader-visible qualification, structure recommendation, and handoff.
- The four-section architecture gives the Pool an independent infrastructure
  section and pairs Leviticus with Gallery around distinct room/interface work.
  This makes comparison legible without narrating chronology as causality.
- The Step-08 reader-readiness check and Step-09 audit moved Source diagnosis,
  selection rationale, and correction mechanics out of reader prose while
  preserving reader-necessary limits on origin claims, representation, place,
  feedback, and the “Heartbeat” oral-history sequence.
- Step 10a retains equivalent overall length to `-01`, but records deliberate
  Event-level structure and what a presentation must not obscure. Gallery (246
  words) and Pool (231) exceed the flexible 150–220-word chapter target because
  each needs several distinct documented movements and a visible limit; this is
  recorded, not treated as a failure.

Interpretation: the #180 and #182 method changes appear to improve the
traceability of reader-oriented structural choices for this Disco replay. They
do not prove that four sections, seven blueprints, or this chapter length will
be right for another topic.
