# Step 07 — Cross-Event Synthesis

## Purpose

Determine what the researched Events prove together and build a route argument
and reader-oriented Event architecture without inventing causal connections.

## Required inputs

- `00-run-brief.md`
- Confirmed `05-cohort-decision.md`
- `06-deep-route-research.md`

## Work

Compare Events side by side. Extract each Event's unique contribution and
classify relationships as documented influence, parallel development,
contrast, structural link, convergence, chronology, or interpretive synthesis.
Separate evidence from interpretation. Test simultaneous developments and the
historical claims implied by sequence, emphasis, marker treatment, and links.

Build relationship and narrative-section records using the shared schemas.
Compare an event-centric structure with an argument-centric structure when both
are plausible. Group Events only when they perform related historical work;
chronology alone is not a grouping reason. Preserve the accepted Event cohort
while testing sections, changes of scale, and transition logic.

After the thesis, relationships, and section assignments are coherent, build
one shared-schema Event narrative blueprint for every accepted Event. Derive
its reader question, opening move, ordered beats, reader-visible uncertainty,
reader-copy structure recommendation, and route handoff from the Event dossier
and accepted synthesis. Review the blueprints together as a route: each Event
must do distinct historical work, multi-Event sections must remain legible, and
handoffs must not turn chronology, parallel development, or interpretive
synthesis into unsupported causality.

The blueprint plans chapter shape for step 08. Do not draft sentences, settle
paragraph breaks or final heading text, or introduce facts, claims,
relationships, or implications that the dossiers and synthesis do not support.

## Output contract

Write `07-cross-event-synthesis.md` with:

- unique contribution of every Event;
- shared-schema relationship records and supporting evidence;
- direct connections separated from synthesis;
- parallel developments and convergence points;
- final working thesis;
- shared-schema narrative sections with Event assignments and grouping reasons;
- one shared-schema Event narrative blueprint for every accepted Event;
- event-centric versus argument-centric assessment and selected structure;
- confirmation that every accepted Event remains assigned exactly once and has
  exactly one blueprint with stable Event and section IDs;
- visual historical claims and constraints;
- remaining claim-level gaps; and
- drafting-readiness verdict.

## Stop condition

Return to step 05 or 06 when synthesis exposes a cohort or evidence failure.
Stop for Human confirmation when synthesis materially changes the accepted
route direction. Do not advance to step 08 while an accepted Event lacks a
complete, evidence-compatible blueprint.
